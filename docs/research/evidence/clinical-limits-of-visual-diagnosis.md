# Clinical limits of visual diagnosis

- Type: evidence
- Summary: visible signs are nonspecific warnings, some important infections
  are subclinical, and a camera cannot confirm a pathogen.
- Why we care: this is the scientific basis for the product boundary: triage
  and shortlisting, never diagnosis.

## Signs overlap and are not specific

USDA "how to spot sickness" `[read]`
[https://www.aphis.usda.gov/livestock-poultry-disease/avian/defend-the-flock/how-to-spot-sickness](https://www.aphis.usda.gov/livestock-poultry-disease/avian/defend-the-flock/how-to-spot-sickness)

- Visible indicators include changes in energy, appetite, movement, breathing,
  head/comb, production, and greenish watery diarrhea.
- USDA presents them as possible indicators of avian influenza or other avian
  diseases. They are warning signs, not diagnoses.

WOAH Newcastle disease `[read]`
[https://www.woah.org/en/disease/newcastle-disease/](https://www.woah.org/en/disease/newcastle-disease/)

- Signs vary widely: respiratory signs, depression, nervous signs, diarrhea.
- Can resemble avian influenza; laboratory testing is needed to confirm.
- Virulent Newcastle disease is WOAH-listed. Legal reporting duties vary by
  pathogen/pathotype and jurisdiction.

USDA APHIS avian influenza `[read]`
[https://www.aphis.usda.gov/livestock-poultry-disease/avian/avian-influenza](https://www.aphis.usda.gov/livestock-poultry-disease/avian/avian-influenza)

- HPAI signs can include sudden death, reduced energy/appetite, egg changes,
  head/comb swelling or discoloration, respiratory signs, torticollis,
  stumbling, and diarrhea.
- LPAI may produce no signs.
- APHIS directs owners who observe suspicious illness to contact a
  veterinarian or animal-health authority promptly. Formal reporting duties
  depend on disease classification and jurisdiction.

## Some diseases are subclinical

MSD Veterinary Manual, common infectious diseases in backyard poultry `[read]`
[https://www.msdvetmanual.com/exotic-and-laboratory-animals/backyard-poultry/common-infectious-diseases-in-backyard-poultry](https://www.msdvetmanual.com/exotic-and-laboratory-animals/backyard-poultry/common-infectious-diseases-in-backyard-poultry)

- Common paratyphoid Salmonella infections are often subclinical. A camera-
  only Salmonella diagnosis, or a visually “negative” result claiming absence
  of infection, is therefore not defensible. Symptomatic visual associations
  may be researched, but they cannot establish infection status.

## Coccidiosis has signs, but they are not exclusive

MSD Veterinary Manual, coccidiosis `[read]`
[https://www.msdvetmanual.com/poultry/coccidiosis-in-poultry/coccidiosis-in-poultry?query=broiler](https://www.msdvetmanual.com/poultry/coccidiosis-in-poultry/coccidiosis-in-poultry?query=broiler)

- Coccidiosis can cause poor growth, reduced intake, weight loss, diarrhea,
  and mortality. These signs are nonspecific.
- Confirmation uses fecal examination/microscopy together with lesion and
  flock context; mixed infections are common, and some relevant lesions are
  internal and invisible to the camera.

## Dropping appearance is a clue, not a diagnosis

University of Guelph Animal Health Laboratory guidance `[read]`
[https://www.uoguelph.ca/ahl/content/diagnosing-enteric-disease-backyard-chickens](https://www.uoguelph.ca/ahl/content/diagnosing-enteric-disease-backyard-chickens)

- Diagnostic guidance distinguishes normal loose cecal droppings from
  diarrhea and connects some appearances to conditions worth investigating.
- Confirmation still uses fecal parasitology or culture, PCR, histology, or
  postmortem examination as appropriate.
- The product may display measured color or texture and recommend review or
  sampling. It must never translate one color directly into a disease label.

## Rabies correction

An earlier draft disease list for this project included rabies. WOAH `[read]`
[https://www.woah.org/en/disease/rabies/](https://www.woah.org/en/disease/rabies/) states that rabies is a disease of
mammals. It is not a meaningful chicken-camera target and has been removed.
The correction matters because any disease list shipped in product materials
must be species-appropriate.

## Consequence for the product

Our inference: the system can flag "something looks wrong here" with
evidence, and can shortlist birds or zones. It must not output disease names
as findings. Suspected high-consequence or legally reportable disease must be
escalated promptly to a veterinarian or animal-health authority. Isolation
and biosecurity actions follow their guidance while testing is pending;
laboratory testing confirms the diagnosis. See [../PAPER.md](../PAPER.md) and
[../../BIBLE.md](../../BIBLE.md).
