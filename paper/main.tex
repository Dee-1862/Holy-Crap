\title{Held-out Accuracy Is Not New-Collection Accuracy:\\
A Cross-Collection Benchmark of Poultry Dropping Classifiers\\
and a Fault-Isolated Pen-Camera Detector on a QNX Raspberry Pi 5}

\begin{abstract}
Deep networks classify photographs of poultry droppings into healthy, coccidiosis, salmonella and Newcastle disease
with reported accuracies of 98 to 99\%, but these numbers come from random splits of a single collection. We retrained
the classifiers earlier work used (MobileNetV2 and EfficientNet-B0) and our YOLO11n detector on
the same split of the public Tanzanian farm photographs. We then tested all of them on the same 679 held-out farm
photographs and on 1,255 photographs from a separate collection whose labels were confirmed by PCR. Every model scores
98 to 99\% on the farm test and 75 to 76\% on the lab-confirmed photographs, a drop of more than 20 points, so the
test set matters more than the architecture. An audit of the split found 49 test photographs (7.2\%) that are exact
copies of training photographs. Removing them changes the farm accuracy by only 0.1 points, so they do not explain
the gap. The detector's box score ranks photographs well but is not a calibrated probability. We also graded every
lab-confirmed photograph for appearance with three AI raters (Krippendorff's alpha 0.76). Salmonella droppings look
almost healthy, yet both models still name them correctly, while normal looking coccidiosis is mostly missed. Since a fixed pen camera
sees each dropping at 8 to 30 pixels, we fine-tuned the detector on public barn video with real droppings pasted
onto the litter, split by camera. On unseen cameras, recall rises from 0.29 to 0.80 and precision from 0.11 to 0.93,
at a cost of 0.02 mAP50 on close-ups. The full pipeline runs on a Raspberry Pi 5 under the QNX microkernel. Each
stage is an isolated process, and any stage we killed was restarted in 20 to 33\,ms while the others kept running. We
release the code, the per-photograph predictions and every figure with its data.
\end{abstract}

\section*{Data and code availability}
The farm and lab-confirmed photographs are Zenodo records 4628934 and 5801834, the PIO and NESTLER videos Zenodo
records 16686320 and 15063494, all under CC BY 4.0. Our code, trained weights, per-photograph predictions,
duplicate hashes, appearance grades with every rating, training logs and every figure with the table behind it are at \todo{repository URL}. The figures
are produced by \texttt{data-visualization/evaluation.py}, the baselines by
\texttt{training/benchmark\_classifiers.py}, and the image audit by \texttt{data-visualization/audit\_images.py}.

\section*{Ethics}
This study used only published photographs and videos. No animals were handled and no new animal data were collected.
